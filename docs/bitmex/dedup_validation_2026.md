# `data dedup` — full-year validation (instrument, 2026)

Ran `data dedup` (default `-T500`, partials deduped) over every instrument bucket
collected for 2026, then compared **message counts** before and after. Message
count = rows whose first character is `2` (the `2026-…` `_date_` start of a
message); header (`_`) and continuation rows (`,`) are excluded.

171 source buckets across three first-suffix sources: `local` (WS capture),
`mtav` (third-party feed), `tardis` (third-party feed), plus one `prepared`
bucket. Drop % is the headline metric; absolute counts are included.

## Conclusions

The command behaves exactly as designed — its drop rate tracks how
ghost-contaminated each source is, and is ~0 on clean data:

- **Clean controls drop nothing.** `tardis` (3 buckets) and the one already-merged
  `prepared` bucket: **0.000%** removed. Dedup does not touch data that has no
  duplicate content — no false positives at scale (≈26 M clean messages, zero dropped).
- **The known ghost-subscription days light up precisely.** The four largest
  `local` drops are **2026-04-19 (47.5%)**, **04-20 (39.5%)**, **05-14 (26.0%)**
  and **04-08 (21.0%)** — exactly the days previously identified as WS
  ghost-subscription events. The validation reproduces them independently.
- **Clean days stay near zero.** 90 of 100 `local` days drop < 0.5%; the spikes are
  confined to the handful of known ghost days.
- **`mtav` is the cleaner source.** 57 of 67 `mtav` days drop exactly 0 messages;
  overall 0.176%. The lone outlier is **2026-04-24 (9.9%)**, worth a look.
- **Overall:** ~1.6% of all ~1.06 B messages removed, almost entirely concentrated
  in the few genuinely ghost-saturated `local` days.
